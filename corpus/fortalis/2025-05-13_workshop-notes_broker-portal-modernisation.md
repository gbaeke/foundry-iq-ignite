## 1. Goal

Validate the largest theme from the research so far, claims follow-up, and sketch together what a broker needs to see about a claim. The session was bilingual; brokers worked in mixed language groups with Laura and Elien switching between Dutch and French.

As agreed, we opened by repeating that this discovery does not mean a new portal is coming soon.

## 2. Validating the problem

We showed the ticket figure: 41% of helpdesk tickets are "where is my claim?". Reactions:

- "Only 41%? The rest I phone directly." (B07, small office)
- B05 keeps a spreadsheet of every open claim with the date of the last call to Fortalis, and calls every claim older than two weeks.
- B13, a larger office, has one employee spending roughly a day per week chasing claim status for customers.
- The claims handler confirmed the other side: a claim call from a broker takes on average five minutes, and often the answer is "nothing new, the expert hasn't been yet".

Everybody agreed this is the first thing to fix.

## 3. What a broker wants to know

Groups listed questions customers ask their broker after a loss, then ranked them.

| Rank | Question | Votes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has Fortalis received the claim, and who handles it? | 14 |
| 2 | Is an expert coming, and when? | 12 |
| 3 | What is missing from the customer to move forward? | 11 |
| 4 | Is the claim accepted, and how much will be paid? | 9 |
| 5 | When will the payment arrive? | 7 |
| 6 | Can the customer start repairs? | 6 |

Point 3 led to the best discussion: brokers would gladly chase missing documents themselves, if they knew what was missing. Laura noted that her handlers already record missing items, but only internally.

## 4. Sketching

Each group sketched a claim status page on paper. Common elements across all three groups:

- A **timeline** of the claim with plain-language steps (received, handler assigned, expert visit planned, decision, payment).
- The **name and direct contact** of the handler.
- A clear **"waiting for"** block: what is missing and from whom.
- **Notifications** when the step changes, by e-mail, with the option to switch off per claim.
- A **list view** of all open claims of the office, sortable by age and status.

Differences:

- Small offices want e-mail notifications; the larger office wants a daily overview instead of separate messages.
- One group wanted to see the reserve amount. Laura was clear that this will not be shown to brokers.

## 5. Mobile claim reporting

Not on the agenda, but it came up in every group. Brokers visit customers after a loss and want to take photos and start the claim report on the spot. Today they write notes on paper and retype them at the office in the evening. Parked for the roadmap; it fits naturally after claim status.

## 6. Internal side

The claims handler and account manager raised two points to take into account later:

- Status steps must come automatically from the claims process; handlers will not update a second system.
- Brokers must not see internal comments by handlers.
