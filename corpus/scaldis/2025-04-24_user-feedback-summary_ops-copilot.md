## 1. Sources

- Feedback buttons in the answer cards (thumbs up/down with reason)
- Four weekly feedback reviews with Nathalie Dumont
- Eight short interviews on the terminal: three shift leaders, two planners, two gate clerks, one reefer technician
- A two-question survey in Teams (19 of 25 pilot users responded)

## 2. Usage

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Active pilot users (at least one question) | 23 of 25 |
| Questions asked | ~240 |
| Rated helpful (of rated answers) | 75% |
| Share of questions asked between 22:00 and 06:00 | 18% |
| Share asked on phone | 41% |

Night shift usage is higher than we feared (R-11). Several night shift users said they ask the bot "before waking up the shift leader".

## 3. What users like

- **Speed of finding the right document.** "I used to search three sites. Now I ask and click the link." (planner)
- **Steps collapsed.** Shift leaders on the quay read the two-line answer and only open the steps when needed.
- **Language.** French-speaking operators appreciate that they can ask in French over Dutch SOPs.
- **Version date.** Seeing the date makes users more critical of old documents and they report them.

## 4. What users do not like

1. **Outdated French versions.** The most frequent thumbs-down reason. Content owners archived 31 documents during the month; more remain.
2. **No incident procedures.** Shift leaders ask about spill and injury procedures and get partial answers from general SOPs. They understand why, but it is the main gap in their eyes.
3. **Answers too long on phone** when the source is a checklist. We now show the first three checklist items and a "show all" link.
4. **"Ask the shift leader" during shift change.** The roster list sometimes points to the leader of the previous shift. Nathalie's team will update the list at 06:00, 14:00 and 22:00 instead of daily.

## 5. Adoption observations

- Champions emerge naturally: two shift leaders show the bot to new staff during briefings. We propose to formalise this as a "copilot champion" per shift for the rollout.
- Gate clerks on company phones use it less than expected. They have their hands full at the gate; they ask colleagues instead. For them a desktop presence on the gate PCs would matter more (see R-09).
- Planners use it mainly for rare procedures (breakbulk, out-of-gauge). They know the common ones by heart.
